Research Experience

Moral Sycophancy in Vision Language Models

Submitted · ACL ARR Aug 2026

First Author · Supervised by Dr. Irfan Ahmad, KFUPM

Finds that VLMs frequently abandon correct moral judgments once a user disagrees, even without new evidence — and are far more likely to shift from right to wrong than the reverse, revealing a clear agreement bias. Open-source models are substantially more susceptible than proprietary ones. Proposes two training-free mitigations, CoT+Vis and Pressure-Blind Visual Arbitration; CoT+Vis cuts sycophancy below 3% on both Moralise and MM-MoralBench.

Pre-print (arXiv:2602.08311) · Inference cost funded by SDAIA–KFUPM Joint Research Center for AI

Visual Hallucination Reduction: An Input-Level Approach for Multimodal Language Models

Submitted · Multimedia Systems, Springer

Second Author · Supervised by Sabbir Ahmed, IUT

Proposes a model-agnostic, adaptive image-preprocessing method that reduces visual hallucination in LLMs without retraining — choosing noise-reduced, edge-enhanced, or original images per question type. Cuts hallucinations by 44.3%, improving factual grounding and reliability.

Pre-print version

Copy-Move Forgery Detection Utilizing SIFT and FLANN with Optimized Keypoint Matching

ICCIT 2024

Undergraduate Thesis · Supervised by Dr. Sk Md. Masudul Ahsan, KUET

A copy-move forgery detection method using SIFT keypoint extraction and FLANN matching with Lowe's ratio test, classifying an image as forged once at least two keypoint pairs match, and visualizing suspected source and copied regions with colored patches. Reached F1-scores of 95% on MICC-F220 and 81% on MICC-F2000.

Paper (IEEE Xplore) · Best Technical Presentation of the Session Award
